Crypto was an inescapable part of NBC's Super Bowl broadcast this year.

Coinbase opted for a minimalist approach with a commercial featuring a QR code bouncing around like a retro screensaver. Crypto.com's ad featured LeBron James in conversation with his younger self. And in FTX's "Don't Miss Out" commercial, comedian Larry David portrayed a crypto skeptic who has previously been wrong about revolutionary inventions including the wheel, the fork, and the toilet.

The cost of a 30-second ad slot reached around $7 million during this year's big game — but that's small change for large crypto exchanges.

"They're sitting on these massive war chests that they just need to spend," Mason Nystrom, a senior analyst at the research firm Messari Crypto, told Insider. "It's really about exposure — centralized exchanges are competing for users, and the more eyeballs they get, the better chance they have of attracting them."

As well as exchanges, layer-one blockchains have also upped their sports marketing. Avalanche agreed a deal with motorsport titans Andretti last year, while Tezos penned agreements with Manchester United and Red Bull Racing.

The total number of deals between crypto and sports companies grew 1,100% between 2019 and 2021, according to Nielsen's latest global sports marketing report. And Nielsen analysts projected that the blockchain companies will spend $5 billion on sports sponsorship in 2026, up 778% on 2021.

Insider identified 12 crypto leaders and marketing experts who are landing major deals with prominent sports leagues, teams, and athletes. They are listed in alphabetical order, by company.

## Ava Labs Head of Marketing Devon Ferreira

Ava Labs' Avalanche token is currently the 10th-most valuable cryptocurrency by total market capitalization.

Ferreira told Insider that the company's sports marketing strategy aims to drive up its valuation while also helping it to win the so-called layer-1 battle against rival blockchains like Ethereum and Solana.

"I have two simple marketing priorities for Avalanche: driving adoption of our network, and winning the crypto brand war," Ferreira told Insider. "To win that brand war, we have to lean hard into storytelling, which is why we're focussing on sport."

Stops at Disney and Oakley taught South African marketing veteran Ferreira how to use storytelling to build an emotional connection with the community of Avalanche users and developers.

That encouraged him to ink a long-term deal with the Andretti Autosport Formula E team. By tying Avalanche's name to an environmentally-friendly racing franchise, he wanted fans to better-understand the blockchain's core values, which he said are "speed, science, and sustainability".

"We won't win that brand war by forcing technical details about decentralization and utility down people's throats," Ferreira told Insider. "When people hear the word Avalanche, I don't want them to think anything — I want them to feel something."

Avalanche follows Andretti around 10 cities during the course of the Formula E season, setting up a traveling roadshow designed to attract crypto-curious punters. Ferreira has helped to establish events including a "Blockchain 101" seminar before the Mexico City ePrix and a hackathon coinciding with the Berlin race.

"The partnership is about more than just slapping a sticker on the car," he said. "The idea is to build energy, use race day as a way to make some marketing noise and build awareness, but also to engage with the local crypto community."

Ferreira sees Crypto.com's Staples Center deal and Coinbase's Super Bowl ad as watershed moments in establishing a connection between crypto and sports. He told Insider that major sporting brands now approach Avalanche on a daily basis.

"The Crypto.com deal was so important for the industry because it made a lot of people sit up and realize that crypto companies can sign deals of this magnitude," he added. "It's now obvious to sports marketing executives that these companies have money and are in need of more exposure."

## Bybit Head of Communications Igneus Terrenus

The Singapore-based crypto exchange Bybit made headlines in February when it signed a multi-year deal to sponsor Formula One team Red Bull, reportedly worth $150 million. The F1 team told Reuters it was "the largest annual crypto tie-up in international sport" at the time.

Terrenus, who is behind most of Bybit's sports sponsorship deals, told Insider he saw parallels between Red Bull Racing and Bybit's brands.

"Red Bull Racing is basically a department of a fizzy drinks company that entered a car racing competition, when you already have the likes of Ferrari and McLaren," he said. "It's kind of like a middle finger to the establishment, which is something that you would say crypto companies do as well."

Another major reason behind the partnership was the international audience of Formula One. Terreus said most other major crypto exchanges are "fighting it out" in the US market, leaving the rest of the world up for grabs. Formula One's calendar of races across the world allows for Bybit's brand to reach consumers in numerous countries with just one branding partnership.

Bybit is also looking to appeal to more affluent audiences to market its passive earning products. Terrenus said Red Bull Racing "represents kind of a more affluent and well-to-do group of people."

"We're trying to pitch to people who maybe are not looking to be full time hardcore crypto traders," he added. "They just want a wealth management product that performs better than inflation."

Bybit's first major involvement in sports goes back to November 2020, when the company announced a multi-year partnership with German Bundesliga club Borussia Dortmund.

"Football is definitely a mass play, especially in Europe and South America. Almost everyone watches football," Terrenus said.

"Borussia Dortmund is interesting in the sense that virtually every other major European football team, they are very polarizing," he said. "But there are very few Borussia Dortmund haters, even if they don't have as many fans as, say, Manchester United."

Terrenus said sports is an attractive advertising medium for Bybit because of its inbuilt events calendar, captive audience, and reliance on emotion, which predisposes customers to being receptive to branding efforts.

Bybit's relative short list of sports sponsorship deals compared to companies like Crypto.com or FTX stems from the company's careful vetting of potential partners, said Terrenus.

"We understand that every time you sign a partnership, it doesn't matter how great it is, there's always a dilution of what you stand for," he said. "We really just try to make that dilution as minimal as possible."

## Coinbase CMO Kate Rouch

Like rival exchanges Crypto.com and FTX, Coinbase has spent significantly to increase its presence in the world of sports over the past few years.

In October, the Nasdaq-listed firm agreed to become the exclusive crypto partner of the NBA and WNBA. Brooklyn Nets superstar Kevin Durant agreed to become one of the brand's public faces in December. Durant's investment firm Thirty Five Ventures also invested in Coinbase's $100 million funding round back in 2017.

More recently, Coinbase turned heads with its distinctive Super Bowl LVI ad.

The 60-second commercial featured a QR code bouncing around like a retro DVD screensaver. If users activated the code, it took them to a page on the exchange's website offering $15-worth of free bitcoin if they set up an account.

The ad drove huge traffic towards the Coinbase website — so much so that it briefly crashed.

At the center of Coinbase's advertising efforts is its first-ever chief marketing officer Kate Rouch, whom it poached from Facebook in August 2021. Rouch, who spent over a decade at Facebook, drew comparisons between the two companies.

"Coinbase is almost exactly the size Facebook was when I joined," she wrote in a LinkedIn post last year. "It too is led by a visionary founder, in a new space, with a bold mission and huge potential to fundamentally change the world."

## Crypto.com CMO Steven Kalifowitz

From buying the naming rights to the Los Angeles Lakers' stadium for $700 million, to releasing a Super Bowl ad featuring LeBron James, Crypto.com's CMO has had a busy year.

Its roster of sports partnerships now reads like a who's who of the sporting world: the UFC, Formula 1, Italy's top soccer league Serie A, and the South American Football Confederation (CONMEBOL) have all inked deals with the company.

The man behind this marketing blitz, chief marketing officer Steven Kalifowitz, said in an interview with Insider in November 2021 that these moves are meant to build on one another. "You don't just create a relationship with somebody like that," he said of Crypto.com's efforts to raise its profile with consumers. "You've got to develop trust."

Kalifowitz told Insider in December 2021 that he was initially met with silence from potential marketing partners when he first joined the company as CMO in 2020.

"People wouldn't reply," Kalifowitz said. "They were like, 'Who are you guys?'"

But Kalifowitz said public sentiment changed in the following months, amid surging cryptocurrency prices, the stock market debut of crypto exchange Coinbase, and crypto exchange FTX's $135 million naming rights deal for the Miami Heat's stadium.

In the same December interview, he said Crypto.com's stated mission of accelerating "the world's transition to cryptocurrency" is the impetus behind the company's sponsorships.

"Accelerating it means showing people that there's a company out there that's trustworthy, does business the right way, who operates in a regulated and licensed manner and offers safe, secure products," he said.

The year ahead looks just as busy for Crypto.com - the company recently announced it will be an official sponsor of the FIFA World Cup 2022 in Qatar.

## FTX Head of Marketing Nathaniel Whittemore

In just two years, leading crypto exchange FTX went from zero to 100 in sports marketing partnerships.

Now the FTX brand is synonymous with sports, having snapped up deals with the biggest athletes on the planet from star NFL quarterback Tom Brady to basketball legend Steph Curry. It also secured naming rights for the home of The Miami Heat, one of the top sports stadiums in the US, for a reported $135 million.

Its move into sports began with the acquisition of Blockfolio back in 2020, a deal forged with the aim of expanding its reach to more retail traders. FTX head of marketing Nathaniel Whittemore worked as a consultant at Blockfolio and moved into a full-time role after the acquisition.

"It took us a couple months of discussion, but it became pretty clear pretty soon that if we were going to compete for that type of retail investor, that we really needed to do a lot to get our brand out there, to build brand awareness," Whittemore told Insider. "And so it naturally followed from that, leaning on really big partnerships that could help supercharge that brand awareness really quickly."

The team was able to move quickly in securing deals because they had buy-in from the very top. FTX's CEO Sam Bankman-Fried is an intensely passionate sports fan known for having games displayed on at least one of his multiple computer monitors while he works.

"Sam was one of the folks who originally had a very strong instinct towards stadiums," Whittemore said. "Part of the reason being that there is just so much free exposure that comes from that, so many people are experiencing it, broadcast partners who just reference it casually and so you have that repetition."

Plus, he added, FTX's short, three-letter brand is well-suited to an arena, where people won't give it a "shortcut name."

Bankman-Fried also played a key role in pursuing superstar quarterback Tom Brady, Whittemore said.

"We wanted to be partnered with people who had really iconic brands," Whittemore said. "Over the years, our goal is to make FTX an iconic brand, but you can't just come out and say, 'Hey, we're an iconic brand.' You have to go build that."

FTX was among a crop of crypto brands featured in this year's Super Bowl telecast. The spot starred comedian Larry David as a time-traveling crypto skeptic and carried the tagline: "Don't be like Larry. Don't miss out on the next big thing."

"I think that if we make the barrier to entry into crypto being that you're already super sure that crypto is really important, we are stuck with the people that we already have," Whittemore said. "And if we make the barrier to entry super complex or feeling like you have to have that complex knowledge, I think we also lose part of what makes it disruptive."

Whittremore described last year as a "mad dash" to get its sports partnerships set up; now the emphasis is on engaging with the fans at the heart of the partnerships.

## Gemini Chief Executive Tyler Winklevoss and President Cameron Winklevoss

Amid the flurry of crypto companies announcing nine-figure multi-year deals with global sports teams and events, US-based crypto exchange Gemini in January instead sponsored a relatively unknown team: the 10th-tier English football club Bedford FC.

"Gemini could have partnered with an established Premier League club, but sponsoring Bedford FC represents a meaningful opportunity to transform a club through the power of Bitcoin and its community," Tyler Winklevoss, CEO of Gemini, told local newspaper the Bedford Independent.

"Bitcoin has the ability to change the world, and we want to reflect that in where we invest our money," he added.

The crypto exchange's first major foray into sports came last year, when it sponsored the historic university rowing race between Oxford and Cambridge, which adopted the title "the Gemini Boat Race." The sponsorship came as a result of the twin founders' passion for rowing.

As part of the event, Gemini also instituted a bursary to help widen the accessibility and diversity of rowing for young people with limited access to the sport through The Gemini Boat Race Bursary Scheme, said the company.

## NYDIG CMO Kelly Brewster

In November 2021, bitcoin company NYDIG announced a landmark partnership with The Houston Rockets. The NBA team, which is owned by American businessman Tilman Fertitta, will have their sponsorship fee paid in bitcoin, with the proceeds held on NYDIG's bitcoin platform.

This deal creates an opportunity for NYDIG to expand the bitcoin ecosystem through access, educational programming, and community support initiatives in partnership with the Rockets, according to a November press release.

"The Rockets will be #PaidInBitcoin, and this partnership underscores our excitement about the Houston area and the bitcoin innovation taking place there," said Brewster in a November press release. "NYDIG's partnership with the Fertitta family, across their businesses, will significantly accelerate its goal of making bitcoin accessible to all."

Brewster spent several years working in client strategies and marketing for Stone Ridge Asset Management, which spun out NYDIG in 2020. Brewster also had stints at fintech platform Flourish and at Goldman Sachs where he worked in equity solutions. He has a bachelors degree in information management and technology from Syracuse University.

It's not just the Houston Rockets placing its bitcoin payments with NYDIG: retired NFL quarterback Drew Brees is opting to take part of his salary from his role as analyst for NBC Sports in bitcoin through NYDIG's savings plan.

## OKX CMO Haider Rafique

Seychelles-based cryptocurrency exchange OKX made its first foray into sports-entertainment partnerships this year.

The exchange, formerly known as OKEx, nabbed a partnership with Manchester City, the UK football club sitting at the top of the Premier League. In 2021, Forbes named Manchester City the sixth most valuable football team with a total value of $6 billion.

Securing the deal for OKX is Haider Rafique, the firm's chief marketing officer who first kick-started his career in the crypto space as the first head of growth for Blockchain.com.

Rafique describes acquiring the deal as "pure hustle" on their part.

"OKX is built through perseverance and innovation. We look for those ingredients in our partners. We look for winners, who are on the journey to be on the top. This is what excited us the most about Man City," said Rafique in an email.

The Premier League team gives OKX massive brand distribution through a community of fans who are extremely passionate about the sport, and have shown interest in crypto, Rafique said.

OKX is already the second-top crypto exchange for derivatives, according to CoinMarketCap.com. From the partnership, Rafique hopes to see an increase in people onboarded to the platform and the crypto markets alongside helping them make better and more responsible investment decisions.

"The ultimate metric will be: Did we help people achieve their financial goals? Naturally we will capture soft KPIs such as traffic and engagement but that stuff is boring," Rafique said, referring to key performance indicators.

"Ultimately, sports platforms provide OKX and our industry with unique storytelling and engagement opportunities," he added.

## Socios CEO Alexandre Dreyfus

Socios.com sells 'fan tokens' that claim to offer supporters the opportunity to vote on issues that are relevant to their team. As the company's chief executive, Dreyfus has landed deals with high-profile soccer teams including FC Barcelona, Manchester City, and Paris Saint-Germain, as well as 24 NBA franchises.

In July last year, the $3 billion crypto company announced that it had signed a deal to become the main jersey sponsor of reigning Italian champions Inter. It reported at that time that 1.2 million fans had downloaded the Socios.com app.

"This is a special event. It's the first time in more than a quarter of a century that the name on the front of the club's shirt has changed," Dreyfus said in July. "The Inter Milan front of shirt is the perfect space for us to promote the start of this new partnership and build mass awareness of the opportunities for innovation and engagement."

Malta-based Dreyfus previously founded three start-ups, the last of which was an online poker league that he sold to the Nasdaq-listed Scientific Games.

He's stated in the past that Socios' main aim is to monetize sports teams' global fanbases. Leading English Premier League teams can fill stadiums with capacities of around 60,000 on a weekly basis, but have tens of millions of fans worldwide.

"We are not targeting the guy who has a tattoo and is a season ticket holder," Dreyfus told City A.M. last year. "Our clients are the guys who will most likely never go to the stadium and yet dream about the team but were born in Korea, Japan, Turkey, or Brazil."

Fan tokens also offer sports teams an opportunity to engage with a new generation of digitally savvy, crypto-native fans.

## Terraform Labs CEO Do Kwon

On February 1, Do Kwon, the CEO of Terraform Labs — developer of the Terra blockchain network and Anchor — announced to the Terra community that he wanted to spend $40 million to partner with a sports franchise in one of the four major US sports leagues.

For legal reasons, Kwon said he could not share the identity of the franchise with the community until the voting period finished, but he did provide a complete breakdown of what the partnership would entail and what it could offer in terms of brand exposure.

"The deal includes a variety of attractive Terra brand exposure deliveries, ranging from pronounced signage inside the Venue to national broadcast TV exposure and decorating the entire Club with Terra branding," said Kwon in the proposal. He added that Terra would also look to expand the partnership into new areas under an exclusive negotiating window with the franchise.

Less than two weeks later, the proposal was approved by the community and a partnership with the Washington Nationals MLB team was announced.

"By approving this sponsorship deal, the community has a new way to engage and educate the public, including the policy makers doing important work in Washington, D.C., about decentralized money and the burgeoning technology that underlies it." said Kwon in a statement at the time.

Kwon is known for making bold moves in the crypto market. He recently made a $10 million bet on the future price of Terra (LUNA), the company's governance token. Since the end of January, he's also bought $1 billion of bitcoin as reserves for the company's stablecoin UST, according to Bloomberg.

## Voyager Digital CMO Pam Kramer

US-based crypto exchange Voyager Digital became the US National Women's Soccer League first crypto brokerage partner in December 2021, inking one of the largest corporate sponsorships in the league's history, according to sports trade media Sportico.

The company will also set up and fund crypto accounts for players, and provide them with financial education on crypto, designed to help their finances after their competitive playing careers have ended.

Those funds will come from Voyager's investment in the league, with roughly 50% of the payments made to NWSL evenly split by every player on the league's roster, according to Sportico.

"Crypto is about equal opportunity and trust. Our research shows that women are just as likely as men to buy crypto this year, which is why our partnership with the National Women's Soccer League is so important," Kramer told Insider in an email. "While their union has made some remarkable progress in boosting pay and benefits for NWSL players, it still lags well behind other professional sports' organizations."

Earlier in 2021, Voyager agreed to a five-year partnership with the Dallas Mavericks. As part of the deal, Voyager also received naming rights to the Mavs Gaming Hub, the official gaming venue and digital home court of the Mavs' NBA 2K League team.

Kramer said that Mavericks' owner Mark Cuban's prominent position in the crypto world added to the value of the partnership.

"There's a lot of hype, there's a lot of discussion, but most people don't understand the fundamentals behind it. We're going to try to bring that level of education to our fans," said Cuban.
