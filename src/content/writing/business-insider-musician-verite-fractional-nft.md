New York-born singer-songwriter Vérité, real name Kelsey Byrne, counts over 1 million monthly listeners on Spotify and has collaborated with the likes of electronic music producer R3hab and artist-activist collective Pussy Riot.

She is among a handful of artists — including Diplo and Nas — exploring how Web3 can change the artist-fan relationship by bringing more revenue and creative control to musicians.

As an independent artist for much of her career, Vérité said she turned to Web3 platforms and NFTs to contend with the "algorithmic gating" of current social media and streaming platforms.

"It was increasingly frustrating that some of the content that I post for people who've opted into my ecosystem doesn't get to them unless I pay," she said, referring to having to pay for ads on those platforms.

She first experimented using non-fungible tokens in April 2021, when she auctioned off a percentage of her streaming royalties as an NFT for her song "By Now''. She set the valuation to $1 million dollars and wound up selling 3.3% of that for $33,000, according to the Zora crypto marketplace.

However, she realized that the relatively high price point of her NFTs, which have ranged from $1,800 to $20,000, was putting off a lot of her fanbase from participating.

That's when she came across Royal, a platform that lets users own NFTs of an artist's streaming royalties earned from a song. She was able to "slice" her streaming royalties and make each slice into a smaller, more affordable NFT, with some starting at $145. Her drop sold out in seconds and raised approximately $90,000, according to the breakdown on Royal.

The startup was cofounded by Justin Blau, a DJ and producer also known as 3lau, and has backing from Web3 heavyweight venture capital firms including a16z and Paradigm. It is among a slew of Web3 music platforms, like Catalog and Sound.xyz, that aim to give artists more control over their music and fanbase, while also improving the fans' experience.

As a fan, "you're putting time and effort and money into an artist anyway. We just give you the ability to connect with the artist and to potentially experience upside," Blau told Insider.

"So many people actually have the opposite experience as an artist becomes more popular. Tickets get more expensive, it's harder to get into shows, less of a personal relationship is developed," he added.

Vérité said artists need to embrace the fact that to thrive in the Web3 music landscape, they need to think like an entrepreneur, and craft their own narratives and communities. "There's not a lot of support. You're not reliant on the platform to do the work," she said.

While major record labels like Universal Music Group are taking steps to partner with NFT platforms, for now Vérité sees artists using NFTs as an independent play. "It's not really beneficial to the labels. None of this is," she said. "Just in the same way streaming wasn't beneficial initially."

But NFTs can have a bad reputation among consumers, with million-dollar scams regularly making headlines.

"When there's a lot of money floating around, people get excited," Vérité said. That's why she made sure to frame the NFTs sold through Royal as the "next generation of a merch bundle," to avoid people seeing this as an investment or feeling like shareholders in her career.

"You're getting a percentage of your favorite song, which is really a sentimental act, not a financial act," she said. Vérité also bundled physical cassettes and access to exclusive experiences as part of the drop, in order to "create a value floor so that people couldn't be disappointed".

"If you frame it this way, maybe less people will buy it, but the quality of people that are buying it are buying it for the reasons that you want them to," she added.

Vérité does believe there will be a point when one of the songs dropped on a Web3 platform like Royal will hit a billion streams, which will lead to "people seeing meaningful money" from their NFTs. But she cautioned that getting to a billion streams is an anomaly, and urged fans to take a practical perspective.

"It's not about the money. You love the thing and get to be a part of it," she said. "You're a patron of that artist."

Update: Royal helps artists sell NFTs to let fans own a fraction of their music royalties. A previous version of this article incorrectly stated that Royal helped artists sell fractional NFTs.
